I Origin

I learned people through text before I learned them in rooms.

In my teens I lived in chats — the kind of quiet, always-on world that introverts built for themselves before it was called anything. Reading between someone's messages, noticing what they didn't say, catching the pause before the reply — these became the first instruments I learned to use.

Later I became an engineer. iOS at IBM, then Sberbank, then leading mobile at SberDevices, then platform teams at Yandex Eats, then digital banking at MTS. The formal path is on LinkedIn. The real path is that the same instrument kept working — reading the pause between what a team says and what a team means.

Some of the most useful work I've done was noticing a quiet, capable colleague who wouldn't put themselves forward, and spending a few months helping them become visible.

I care about the questions the field is beginning to ask — about consciousness, empathy, and what these systems we're building actually are. I care about Japan, dark beauty, careful craft, and the idea that a thing worth making is worth making slowly.

III Work

Four chapters. The formal résumé is elsewhere.

i.
2017 – 2020 · IBM, Sberbank

Craft

iOS engineer on Sberbank Online — the app serving twelve million people. Objective-C giving way to Swift. Open-source contributions to OCMock. First conference talks. First article on Habr, which is still cited when people search "custom instruments" in Russian.

What I learned: the craft rewards patience. And the best way to help another engineer is to write the résumé they wouldn't write for themselves.

ii.
2020 – 2022 · SberDevices

Building from zero

iOS team lead, then Head of Mobile for Smart Home. Fifteen people. Shipped the Smart Home feature from kickoff to App Store in 3.5 months. Then shipped the standalone Salute Smart Home app on both stores. Vendor exits, sanctions, supply disruption — sourced replacements without breaking the roadmap.

What I learned: building something from nothing is easier than fixing something that's frozen. The hardest part is hiring the first five people right.

iii.
2023 – 2024 · Yandex Eats

Platform

Three platform teams — mobile, web, automation — supporting food delivery across CIS, Africa, Russia. Cut native release cycles in half. Moved web to daily modular releases. Raised regression automation to 70%. Founded the Mobile Performance Guild, which is still active after I left.

What I learned: the platform you build for other teams is judged not by its features but by the pauses it removes from their day.

iv.
2024 – 2026 · MTS Bank, Transneft

Reckoning

Tech lead of digital banking at MTS — a cross-functional org of around 170 across eight teams — through a period of high external pressure and heightened security requirements. Introduced AI tooling that cut routine engineering effort by 20-30%. Then Head of Development at Transneft, building processes for a 45-person team from ground up in nine months.

What I learned: a well-argued problem isn't automatically a solvable one. Some problems are structural, and part of leadership is recognizing that pattern early enough to save your energy.

V Notes

Small things I'm thinking about.

Draft

On quiet colleagues

The most useful thing a senior person can do is notice a junior one who won't put themselves forward, and spend attention on them anyway. This scales badly. It's still worth it.

Draft

Well-argued isn't the same as solvable

Sometimes the problem is not that the argument is wrong. The problem is that fixing it would require someone to admit their own earlier hiring call was off. That's a different kind of problem.

Draft

Dark beauty as a working principle

Something can be dark and considered at once. Precision in tone. Restraint in ornament. Room for one clear moment of softness. This is a working principle, not an aesthetic.
